How to read this

  • Low Predicted core temperature below 101.7°F. Your dog should cool down normally. Fine for a walk with the usual water and breaks.
  • Moderate Predicted core temperature 101.7°F – 104.2°F. Your dog stays warm for longer after exercise. Keep it shorter and slower, stick to shade, bring water and watch for heavy panting.
  • High Predicted core temperature above 104.2°F. Heat stress is likely. Skip the walk or keep it to a quick, shaded potty break.

Each column shows how warm your dog is likely to be 15, 30 and 60 minutes after about 15 minutes of hard exercise. Longer exercise can warm your dog more. Dogs are always warm right after exercise, so Moderate at 15 minutes is common. What matters most is whether it drops to Low by 60 minutes; the best times above use the 60-minute column.

Use caution if you see High in the 15 or 30 minute column. It means that after 15 or 30 minutes of recovery, your dog is experiencing heat stress.

Weather data by Open-Meteo.com. Guidance only, not veterinary advice. The model is from my IST707 project: it learned from a small study of working dogs in three months of the year, and doesn't know your dog's coat, hydration, shade or pavement temperature. When in doubt, go shorter and bring water.
